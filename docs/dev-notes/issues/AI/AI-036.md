## Description

During owner acceptance testing, the computer repeatedly called Razzle-Dazzle. Audit its eligibility, selection mixture, recent-call handling, turnover risk and situational use. The owner has now specified a normal coaching target of roughly one to three Razzle-Dazzle calls per team per full game, used as surprise plays. Evaluate excessive repeated use against that explicit product target. Frequent legal calls alone do not establish a rules defect; separate call-selection quality from the already corrected pending Razzle-Dazzle result-choice behavior in AI-033.

## Resolution / verification

- Treat Razzle-Dazzle as a rare situational surprise call, with the owner's normal target of roughly one to three calls per team per full game. This is a coaching preference, not a new football rule or restriction on human legal actions. Do not force calls merely to meet a minimum; justify and review unusual usage outside the normal range.
- Separate special-play weighting from ordinary passing choices and account for recent use so that a repeated Razzle-Dazzle sequence is discouraged. Verify ordinary, late-half and fourth-down selection paths against the same intent without duplicating engine rules.
- Measure actual call counts and denominators, runs of repeated calls, comparable public situations and resolved outcomes; keep detailed historical play evidence private.
- Inspect Medium ordinary and late-game selection, including whether Razzle-Dazzle shares an ordinary pass bucket and whether repetition penalties apply to that path; compare other difficulties and frozen policy versions without changing them.
- If a policy defect is demonstrated, add synthetic red-first tests, preserve hidden-call and future-dice boundaries, introduce a new policy revision as needed, and validate focused and complete-game coaching audits before resolving; validate the owner's normal-use target with a distribution of complete synthetic games and preserve situational judgment rather than asserting an unsupported hard quota.
